# Orca Privacy Policy

> **Draft, not final.** This policy has not been reviewed by a lawyer. It must be reviewed by a qualified person before Orca's public launch. It describes the friends-only test version of the app.

**Last updated:** November 17, 2026

Orca is an app where friends post their meals, workouts, sleep, steps, wake-up time and screen time, rate each other's meals and workouts, and compete on scores, goals and badges. This policy explains what Orca collects, who can see it, and how you can remove it.

## Who runs Orca

Orca is made and run by an individual developer in Massachusetts, USA (not a company). In this policy, "Orca", "we" and "us" mean that developer.

## The short version

- Only your friends see what you post. Nothing you post is public.
- Orca reads Apple Health only if you connect it, only the types you switch on, and never writes to it. Raw Health data stays on your iPhone. Orca stores only daily totals and the workouts you choose to post.
- Health data is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared with anyone except your friends, unless the law requires it, as described below.
- Ratings are shown without names: Orca never shows who gave which rating. A 🧢 (calling out a screen-time number) is named.
- Orca has no ads and no third-party analytics or tracking.

## What Orca collects

**Your account.** When you sign in with Apple, Orca receives an identifier for your Apple account, your email address if you choose to share it (which may be an Apple relay address), and your name, if you share it, to fill in your profile. Orca uses the identifier and email address only to sign you in.

**Your profile.** Your display name, your username and, if you add them, your profile photo and a short bio (up to 150 characters).

**Your agreement.** When you first open Orca you confirm you are 18 or older and agree to the Terms and this Privacy Policy. Orca stores that you did, the time, and the version of the terms. Before you connect Apple Health, Orca asks for your consent and stores the time you gave it. Nothing from Apple Health is stored for your account until you have given it.

**Your friends.** Who you've sent friend requests to, who has sent them to you, and who your friends are.

**What you post.** Each post is stored with the time it was posted:

- Meal and workout photos, with an optional caption. Orca stores when each photo was taken, and a fingerprint of the image (a content hash) so the same photo can't be posted twice. Photos are compressed on your iPhone before upload.
- The ingredients you add to a meal (each food, its amount, and the calories and nutrients Orca works out from it), and any foods you make yourself (a name, a serving and its nutrition). This is optional.
- Numbers you type (sleep, wake-up time, steps), and any tracker screenshot you attach as proof.
- Screen time, always with a screenshot from Settings → Screen Time.

**Apple Health, if you connect it.** You choose which types Orca reads: steps, sleep (which also gives your wake-up time), and workouts. Your iPhone works out the totals, and Orca stores only:

- your daily step total, minutes asleep and wake-up time;
- for each workout you choose to post: its type, duration, distance and average heart rate, and an identifier from Health so the same workout isn't posted twice.

Orca ignores data you typed into the Health app by hand. Raw Health samples (each step count, heart-rate reading or sleep stage) never leave your iPhone.

**Screenshots.** The text in your screenshots is read on your iPhone (Apple's text recognition) to check the number you posted. The screenshot is uploaded with the post so your friends can check it too.

**Your time zone.** Your iPhone reports its time zone, so your day starts and ends at your own midnight.

**Friends' answers.** When a friend confirms (✅) or caps (🧢) your typed screen time, Orca stores their answer.

**Ratings.** When you rate a friend's meal or workout from 1 to 10, Orca stores your rating with the item and when you gave it. Ratings are blind (you can't see an item's grade until you've rated it or the day is over) and shown without names: Orca never shows anyone, including the person you rated, who gave which rating. With only a few raters, someone may be able to guess. Orca uses stored ratings to work out grades and scores, and to check that raters are fair.

**Reactions, comments and mentions.** The reactions, comments, replies and @-tags you write on posts and day recaps, and when you wrote them. Friends who can see the post can see them, with your name.

**Scores, goals, streaks and badges.** Orca works out a score for each of your days and stores it, along with the weekly goals you pick, each week's result, your streak, and the badges you earn. Your friends can see these on your profile and on their Board.

**Reports and blocks.** If you report a post, comment, recap or person, Orca stores what you reported, who reported it, and the reason if you wrote one (up to 280 characters). If something is held after several reports (see **Blocking and reporting**), Orca stores when it was held and, if it's brought back, when. Orca also stores who you have blocked.

**Notifications.** If you allow notifications, Orca stores your phone's push token (a code Apple and Expo's push service use to reach your phone) and your notification switches. Pushes are sent through Expo's push service and Apple's. Each contains the text of the notification, such as a score, a friend's name or a short excerpt of a comment, and a link to the screen it opens. Orca also keeps an activity list of requests, mentions, replies and reactions in the app.

## Who can see what

- **Your friends** (people you've both accepted) see your profile and your posts: photos, captions, numbers, screenshots, daily totals and posted workout summaries. They never see your raw Health data. They also see your friends list (except people who've blocked them or whom they've blocked, and accounts that are on hold or banned).
- **Meal nutrition:** your friends see the ingredients and nutrition of meals you add ingredients to. A food you make yourself is found only by you; friends see its name and numbers only on your posts that use it. Anyone can choose to hide friends' nutrition in Settings → Privacy.
- **People who aren't your friends** can find you by name or username to send a friend request. They see your private profile: your display name, username, profile photo, bio, how many friends you have, and the friends you have in common with them. They can report your name, username, photo and bio (see **Blocking and reporting**). They can't see your posts, scores or friends list.
- **Confirmations:** your friends see how many friends confirmed your screen time (✅), and the names of anyone who capped it (🧢). If you cap a friend's screen time, they see your name.
- **Scores and the Board:** your friends see your daily score, your profile score, your goals and progress, your streak and your badges, and rank you among themselves on the Board. In Settings → Board you can turn the Board off for yourself, which hides it and your rank from you; your friends still see you on theirs.
- **Ratings:** Orca never shows who gave a rating. Friends see an item's grade once they've rated it or the day is over.
- **Comments and reactions:** anyone who can see the post can see who wrote them.
- **Nobody else.** Photos and screenshots are stored privately and shown only to you and your friends, through links that expire.

## How Orca uses your data

Only to run the app: to show your posts to your friends, work out your scores and ranks, award badges and track goals, send the notifications you've switched on, check that proof is real (photo capture times, screenshots, duplicate photos), and keep your account signed in. Orca does not sell your data, show ads, build advertising profiles, or track you across other apps and websites.

## Apple Health

Orca follows Apple's rules for HealthKit apps:

- Health data is used only to post your daily totals and workouts in Orca.
- It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never given to data brokers or other third parties.
- It is never stored in iCloud by Orca.

You can switch each type off, or disconnect Apple Health, in Orca under Settings → Privacy → Apple Health. To remove Orca's access completely, go to the iPhone's Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Orca. Numbers you've already posted stay, because posts in Orca are final.

## Blocking and reporting

- **Blocking.** You can block a person from their profile. Blocking removes your friendship, hides each of you from the other's search, stops friend requests and @-tags both ways, and hides the blocked person's comments, replies, reactions and activity from you. They are not told. Unblock under Settings → Privacy → Blocked people.
- **Reporting.** You can report a post, comment, recap or person, with an optional reason. Your friends can report what you post. Anyone who can see your profile, friends or not (people who find you in search, or who have a friend request with you), can report your name, username, profile photo and bio. A reported post or comment disappears for you, for good if it's removed, and otherwise for at least 90 days after the report is resolved. Orca's operator gets a notification and reviews each report within 24 hours. Content that breaks the Terms can be removed, and accounts can be banned. A removed post's photos and caption are hidden from everyone at once, and its files are deleted within 24 hours, unless the law requires keeping them (see **When the law requires it**); the day's score stays. Orca never tells anyone who reported them or how many people did.
- **Automatic holds.** When several different people report the same post, comment, recap or person within a day, Orca hides it automatically: a hold. Reports from accounts less than a day old don't count toward it. The hold is automatic, but a person always reviews it, within 24 hours, and either removes it for good or brings it back as it was. While something is held:
  - **A post** is hidden from everyone, with its photos, caption, comments and reactions. Its owner sees "This post is hidden while it's reviewed." Its files stop being shown but are kept until the review. If it's removed, they're deleted within 24 hours, unless the law requires keeping them; if it's brought back, so are they.
  - **A comment** shows as deleted to everyone. Its author sees "Hidden while it's reviewed".
  - **A day recap** is hidden from the owner's friends, with its comments and reactions. Its owner still sees it.
  - **A person** is hidden from everyone: search, friend requests, friends lists, the Board, the Feed, and their posts, comments, reactions and recaps. Their account is locked: they're signed out within an hour and can't sign in. Their data, friendships and scores are kept during the hold, and come back if it's lifted (except a friendship with someone who blocked them in the meantime). Only their sign-ins and their phones' push tokens are cleared; signing in again after a release sets them up again. If it's confirmed, the account is banned, which removes their friendships. Someone who is locked out and wants their account deleted can email orcaapp.help@gmail.com.
- **Intimate images.** Anyone, whether or not they use Orca, can ask for an intimate image of them shared without consent to be removed: see "Removing intimate images" in the [Terms](https://benvieten.github.io/orca-legal/terms#removing-intimate-images), also linked in Settings → Privacy. Orca removes it, and known identical copies, within 48 hours of a valid request.
- **Word filter.** Orca refuses certain blocked words in names, usernames, captions and comments.
- **Report records.** Reports are kept until they are resolved, then for 90 days, and then deleted.

## Where your data is stored

Orca's data is stored with Supabase, which provides Orca's database, file storage and sign-in service. Supabase processes it only to run Orca.

**Server logs.** Supabase keeps request logs (with IP addresses) for a short time to run and secure the service. Sign-in records are deleted with your account.

Notifications are delivered through Expo's push service and Apple's. They receive your push token and, for each notification, its text (which can include a short excerpt of a comment) and a link to the screen it opens, and nothing else from your account.

Apart from them, Orca shares your data only as described in **When the law requires it**, below. Each of these services handles it only to provide its service to Orca, and protects it at least as well as this policy describes.

## When the law requires it

Orca discloses data only when the law requires it or to protect someone from serious harm:

- **Child sexual abuse material.** US law requires Orca to report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), with the account that posted it, and to keep that material, and the account's data that gives it context, for one year. Orca keeps them privately and deletes them after that.
- **Legal requests.** Orca shares data with law enforcement or a court only when given a valid legal request, such as a subpoena or court order, and only what that request covers.
- **Danger.** If someone's life or safety is at immediate risk, Orca may share what's needed with emergency services.

## If your data is exposed

If Orca learns that your data, including your Health totals, was seen or shared without your permission, Orca will tell you by email and in the app (or by letter, if you ask for one) within 60 days of finding out, say what happened and what you can do, and notify the authorities the law requires (such as the Federal Trade Commission).

## Changing your mind

- **Apple Health:** switch types off or disconnect in Orca under Settings → Privacy → Apple Health. Orca stops reading Health at once. Signing out or deleting your account also disconnects Apple Health on that iPhone.
- **Notifications:** switch each kind off in Settings → Notifications, or turn them all off in the iPhone's Settings.
- **Everything:** Settings → Account → Delete account (below).

## Keeping and deleting your data

Posts are final inside the app: they can't be edited or deleted one by one, so scores stay fair. Your data is kept while you have an account.

You can delete your account in the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. It happens immediately. It deletes your profile, posts, photos, screenshots, synced numbers, comments, reactions, goals, badges, friend connections, push tokens, sign-in records and the notifications about you, and asks Apple to revoke Orca's Sign in with Apple access. If your account is on hold or banned, you can't sign in to do that: email orcaapp.help@gmail.com and Orca will delete it for you.

What stays: ratings you gave on finished (locked) days remain inside your friends' frozen grades so their scores don't change, but they are linked to no one. Ratings you gave on open days are deleted. Reports you made, and reports about you or anything you posted, are deleted with your account. Data the law requires Orca to keep (see **When the law requires it**) is kept only as long as the law requires, then deleted.

## Age

While Orca is being tested, it's only for people 18 and older. It isn't directed to children, and doesn't knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. Orca asks you to confirm this when you first open it. If you think someone under 18 has an account, email the address below and it will be deleted.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. Significant changes will also be shown in the app.

## Contact

Questions or requests about your data: **orcaapp.help@gmail.com**

### For the reviewer before public launch

These points need a qualified person's review:

1. The minimum age, and whether a parent's consent flow is needed.
2. Rights under GDPR, the UK GDPR and US state laws (access, export, correction, deletion), and how requests are answered.
3. The legal basis for processing Health data, which is a special category under GDPR.
4. Where Supabase stores data, and the data processing agreements with Supabase and Expo.
5. Retention periods, including the 90 days reports are kept after they are resolved.
6. Whether Terms of Service are needed, and how they link to this policy.
7. Whether Orca is a vendor of personal health records under the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule, and the breach notice wording.
8. The disclosure section (NCMEC reports under 18 U.S.C. § 2258A, legal requests, emergencies).
